    content: I got into VJing at 21, through a [Modul8](https://www.modul8.ch/) workshop run by [Pixelvärk](https://pixelvark.com/) in Stockholm. From there I ran a VJ collective in Montreal called APM300 with [Thien Vu Dang](https://www.instagram.com/thienvudang/) and [Charlotte Risch](https://www.charlotterisch.com), two fantastic video artists, and that projection work was part of how I ended up at [Moment Factory](https://momentfactory.com/). NAVA continued the same thread. When we started in 2016, Aavistus in Helsinki and Nonagon in Sweden did not exist, and neither did most of the audiovisual festivals that came after. We were one of several small initiatives across the Nordics pushing for the art form, and while we had no direct hand in what grew up alongside us, it felt good to be part of an early wave.

  - type: text
    fontSize: small
    content: Through open calls NAVA paired musicians with visual artists, starting at Sónar Reykjavík and the Countdown Stage at [Roskilde Festival](https://www.roskilde-festival.dk/) from 2016 to 2019. In April 2019, after Sónar Reykjavík was cancelled, we and [Plútó](https://www.instagram.com/plutoreykjavik/) put on Skaðablót at [Bíó Paradís](https://bioparadis.is/) in a few weeks, a one-day festival with DJ sets from Phil Hartnoll of Orbital and Kero Kero Bonito, proceeds going to an emergency fund for Icelandic electronic musicians. We also ran [Tiny/Massive](/tinymassive/) at the Reykjavík Winter Lights Festival.

    content: "In 2022, Nordic Lights paired twelve artists into six new audiovisual acts across three festivals: [Monument](https://mnmt.no/) in Norway, [Nonagon](https://nonagonfestival.se/) in Sweden and [Aavistus](https://www.aavistusfestival.fi/) in Helsinki. Residencies ran first: a mountain cabin above a Norwegian valley, a disused steel silo in the Swedish forest, and a wooden farmhouse in the Finnish countryside. Each duo spent a week together writing the piece, then we brought the six new acts to each festival over the summer and autumn."
