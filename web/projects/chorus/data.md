  - type: text
    content: A sound-reactive work projected onto Uppsala Cathedral for [Allt ljus på Uppsala](https://alltljuspauppsala.se/) in 2021 and onto Storkyrkan for [Nobel Week Lights](https://nobelweeklights.se/) the year after.
  - type: text
    fontSize: small
    content: Tove Alderin reached out through our mutual friend [Sophia Wood](https://studiosophiawood.com/). The piece played *Dona nobis pacem* alongside NASA's "Chorus", a VLF recording of electromagnetic waves in the Earth's magnetosphere. Anyone standing outside could sing into their phone and become a voice in the work. Pitch analysis at the Stockholm University Phonetics Laboratory drove particles that formed a pattern across the facade.
  - type: text
    fontSize: small
    content: Both years we worked out of a freezing cargo container on the square, aligning the mapping as our breath fogged the laptops.
  - type: text
    fontSize: small
    content: The tricky part was making hundreds of simultaneous voices still feel personal. With anyone in the city able to join from a phone, each singer needed to recognise their own particle, a few pixels moving the way their voice moved.
  - type: text
    fontSize: small
    content: The work was created by [Tove Alderin](https://www.tovealderinstudio.se/). I handled the projection mapping, composition and socket data. Mattias Heldner at the Stockholm University Phonetics Laboratory did the pitch analysis, [Sol Sarratea](https://solsarratea.world/) wrote the shaders, [David Giese](https://davidgiese.com/) did the video, and [Scenteknik](https://scenteknik.se/) ran the rig in Stockholm.
